Digital Marketing Analytics in Practice

A repeatable plan → collect → analyse → report process, plus the craft of data visualisation.

Prof. Kevin Hartman4 modulesM1 The art of analytics · M2 Data visualisation · M3 Bellabeat case study · M4 The future of analytics

Summary

The hands-on companion to Analytics Theory. It gives a four-step analytics process (plan, collect, analyse, report) and the specifics of each: picking the marketing objective, gathering data, the five pattern types, and telling a story people will actually remember.

A full module is devoted to data visualisation: what separates good visual form from bad (clear meaning, sophisticated contrast, refined polish) and how to build dashboards that aren't 'data pukes'. A Bellabeat case runs the whole process end to end, and the course closes on technology's promise and dark side.

Big idea. Good analysis is a disciplined journey from a clear objective to a visual story, and every detail of the visual carries meaning.

Module 1

The art of analytics

Plan, objective, data, insight, story
Analytics process map
Plan → collect → analyse → report. It is recursive: each pass leaves you with hypotheses and data for the next.
Objectives hierarchy
Business objectives (revenue, volume, profit) are results. Pick one of six marketing objectives: stimulate demand, build awareness, influence consideration, reposition the brand, improve the sales experience, grow loyalty. Then set media and campaign objectives.
Three ways to collect data
Facilitated downloads (preferred), APIs and web scraping. Watch for bias to protect data quality.
Five analysis categories
Descriptive, exploratory, inferential, predictive and causal (the gold standard).
Five pattern types
Change, clustering, relativity, ranking and correlation. Context is often the most important element; beware interpretation bias.
Getting the story straight
People forget fast and get overwhelmed (change-blindness experiments). Reduce mental load and align data to a few key points.

Case / expert

Interviews with a mortgage-company CMO and data partners on sourcing and applying data.

Research insights

  • McKinsey's CDJ used to locate objectives; the McCandless 'information is beautiful' framework of data, story, goal and visual form.

Practical tips

  • Make the objective your North Star; it decides what data you collect.
  • Strategy is a team sport, and data adds perspectives.
Module 2

Data visualisation

Contrast, meaning, polish, dashboards
Good visual form
Three elements: clear meaning, sophisticated use of contrast and refined execution (polish).
Contrast
Size, colour, shape (icons) and designed contrast pull the key data forward and push support data back. Design for colour-blind viewers and sketch on paper first.
Clear meaning
An insightful headline plus a left-aligned subtitle; avoid 'intellectually blank' titles like 'three reasons'. Highlight, label directly and use emotion only when you are sure of the response.
Visual polish
The 2017 Oscars envelope mix-up as a lesson: poor contrast and font choices caused a global error.
Dashboarding
Follow Avinash Kaushik's rules: 3–5 core metrics (never more than 10), insights not just numbers, and owning the story instead of a 'data puke'.

Case / expert

Expert: bad analysis is boring; a number delivered with a point of view can transform an organisation.

Practical tips

  • Every element on the page communicates something, so remove clutter.
Module 3

Bellabeat case study

The process, end to end
Plan
Bellabeat makes design-led health wearables for women (Leaf, Shell, Balance). For a small brand the right objective is awareness, framed through what, how and why questions.
Collect
Find third-party data such as tech adoption over time via Google Dataset Search and assemble it in Excel or Sheets (SQL or R if larger).
Analyse
Throw the data into rough line charts just for yourself to find where the story is.
Report
Strip clutter, add a direct headline, label directly, use icons for shape contrast, keep only what serves the story.

Case / expert

Bellabeat (Ashton Kutcher and Mila Kunis used the Shell to announce their pregnancy).

Practical tips

  • Exploratory charts can be ugly; report charts must be polished.
Module 4

The future of analytics

Promise and dark side
Promise of technology
Exponential processing power, micro-decision data, biometrics and neuroscience, and machine learning finding patterns humans can't.
Dark side I
Bots inflating followers and clicks, click farms, fake news and propaganda.
Dark side II: zombie sites
Machine-built sites recycling bot traffic to steal ad money (the 301Network scheme). Ad fraud was forecast to rise sharply by 2022 while detection lags.
Implications for analysts
Location tracking and data hacks erode privacy. Expect fines, GDPR/CCPA-style legislation and platform self-policing.

Practical tips

  • Validate traffic quality before trusting a metric.

Key takeaways

  1. Follow a repeatable process: plan, collect, analyse, report, then repeat with what you learned.
  2. Revenue is a result; analysis should target one of six marketing objectives.
  3. Prefer facilitated downloads, then APIs, then scraping, and always check for bias.
  4. Look for change, clustering, relativity, ranking and correlation, and give each its context.
  5. Good charts need clear meaning, deliberate contrast and polish.
  6. Dashboards: 3–5 metrics with insights, never a data puke.
  7. Technology brings huge power and real fraud and privacy risks.
