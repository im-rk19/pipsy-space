Digital Media and Marketing Principles

Marketing as communication, and how media moved from mass broadcast to mass personalisation.

Prof. Mike Yao4 modulesM1 Marketing as communication · M2 Digital communication & its impacts · M3 Finding & reaching audiences · M4 Mobile & beyond the screen

Summary

This course reframes marketing as a communication process: every P, even price, sends a message to a receiver who interprets it. From that lens it traces how media evolved from a few powerful mass-media outlets to a digital ecosystem where brands can talk to each person individually.

It then explains what is distinctive about digital communication, how it changes the three goals of marketing communication (information, persuasion, branding), and the toolkit for finding and reaching audiences: targeting, programmatic buying, search, social, mobile and out-of-home.

Big idea. Digital media shifted marketing from one-way mass communication to two-way mass personalisation, and from persuasion to relationship building.

Module notes

Module 1

Marketing as communication

Media revolution and the marketing funnel
Communication lens
Look past channels and tactics to who says what, to whom, why and with what effect. Even pricing is a message about quality and value.
Relationships over persuasion
Communication runs both ways. Winning back a lost customer costs 5–6× more than acquiring one, so digital marketing should build relationships.
Mass communication → mass personalisation
Mass media was the efficient way to reach big markets, but touchpoints were disconnected. Programmatic, behavioural targeting and automation now let brands be relevant to each person at scale, like a restaurant owner who knows every regular.
Marketing funnel & PESO
Awareness → interest → desire → action as a planning tool. Media are paid, owned, earned (and shared); the aim is to combine them, not choose one.

Case / expert

Expert panel: treat paid/owned/earned as one combined system, focused on why and what you are communicating.

Practical tips

  • Ask what message each touchpoint sends, not just which channel to buy.
  • Be there at the right moment of the consumer's journey and stay in touch afterwards.
Module 2

Digital communication & its impacts

Information, persuasion and branding
Five features of digital communication
Interactive, multimedia/vivid, networked, personalised and mobile/always on. Interactive content is more engaging and perceived as more credible.
Information dissemination
Power shifted from media owners (supply side) to consumers (demand side). Brands can publish their own content to anyone, anytime.
Persuasion
Source credibility moved from institutions to individuals: an influencer post can match a TV ad, and one angry review can dent a brand. 'The medium is the message' (McLuhan).
Attention scarcity
Collective and individual attention spans are shrinking as people juggle about five devices; multi-screening weakens selective attention.
Branding & context marketing
Brand personality is built by cognitive association. Context marketing adds timing and personal history to content (the free ice-cream coupon at noon on a hot day).

Case / expert

Expert view: storytelling is back, and the authenticity of the messenger now matters as much as the message.

Research insights

  • Microsoft Canada consumer-insights study on attention spans and multi-screening.

Practical tips

  • Design for interaction, not exposure.
  • Choose authentic messengers; younger audiences want real users.
Module 3

Finding & reaching audiences

Targeting, programmatic, search, social
Audience theories
Uses & gratifications (people pick media to meet needs) and media system dependency (the more someone depends on a medium, the stronger its effect).
Targeting methods
Demographic, contextual, behavioural, geo, retargeting and look-alike targeting, which uses machine learning to find people similar to your best customers.
Programmatic advertising & RTB
An impression is one ad view. Inventory sold mostly by CPM is now bought automatically through ad exchanges via real-time bidding, and the model is spreading to TV and outdoor.
Search marketing
Search reveals intent. SEM covers paid listings plus SEO; pick keywords broad enough to reach interest but narrow enough to stay relevant.
Social media marketing
Platforms built to connect billions; cookie mapping and behavioural targeting let advertisers model who will respond.

Case / expert

Interview with Jeff Green (The Trade Desk): programmatic and data-driven buying is the future of media.

Practical tips

  • Understand why people are on a platform before you interrupt them there.
  • Use look-alike audiences where platforms hold rich profiles.
Module 4

Mobile & beyond the screen

Omnipresence, location, out-of-home
Mobile marketing
Multi-channel reach on phones and tablets via web, SMS/MMS, social and apps.
Technological affordances
Obvious built-in uses (a button is pushed). Seeing the phone only as a 'phone' limits ideas; it is also a computer, TV, tracker, AR display and assistant.
Three mobile affordances
Omnipresence (always within reach), personal two-way communication (felt as part of the self), and location tracking.
Out-of-home goes digital
Billboards, transit and storefronts become programmable with IoT, AR and holograms. Location-based search starts with accurate, machine-readable listings.
Privacy
The question is no longer whether we can collect data but whether we should. Location tactics feel intrusive and that balance tips easily.

Case / expert

Expert view: start from purpose. Don't build a Facebook page just because competitors have one.

Research insights

  • Yao's lab: people who solved tasks on mobile felt smarter and more confident than desktop users.

Practical tips

  • Optimise for small screens and goal-driven, recommendation-reliant users.
  • Keep location data accurate across Google listings and directories.

Key frameworks

Communication modelSource, message, channel, receiver, effect.
Marketing funnelAwareness → Interest → Desire → Action.
PESOPaid, Earned, Shared, Owned media.
Uses & gratificationsAudiences choose media to satisfy needs.

Key takeaways

  1. Treat every marketing action as communication with a purpose and an audience.
  2. The big shift is from mass communication to mass personalisation.
  3. Digital communication is interactive, networked, personalised and always on, and that changes information, persuasion and branding.
  4. Power moved to the demand side: consumers and influencers now hold credibility.
  5. Attention is the scarce resource, so context and timing matter as much as content.
  6. Mobile's real power lies in its affordances: omnipresence, intimacy and location.
  7. Respect privacy, because trust is a business asset.
