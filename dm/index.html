Digital Marketing Specialization: study notes

One page per course with module-by-module concepts, cases, research and takeaways. This overview pulls the whole specialization together: what each course is about, how they connect, and what to remember.

Summary

The specialization moves from why to how to how much. Course 1 rethinks the 4Ps for a world where customers co-create everything. Courses 2 and 3 treat marketing as communication: first the principles of digital media, then a full campaign playbook from planning to KPIs. Courses 4 and 5 are the analytics pair, with theory (mindset, channels, measurement science) and then practice (a four-step process and data-visualisation craft). Course 6 steps back to ask when digital wins, when analog resists, and how the two combine.

Two instructors' threads run through it. Rindfleisch (Courses 1 and 6) focuses on consumers and markets. Yao (2 and 3) focuses on media and communication, and Hartman (4 and 5) on data and measurement.

Key pointers and takeaways by course

Course 1

Marketing in a Digital World

Big idea: Digitisation and democratisation are moving customers from passive buyers to active co-producers across all 4Ps, so firms must change their roles too.

  • The 4Ps still frame marketing, but each one is now executed with digital, democratised tools.
  • Customers have become co-creators of products (Threadless, LEGO Ideas) and of promotion (UGC, GoPro).
  • Critics can co-create too: doppelgänger brand images are free market research if you watch for them.
  • Channels are shortening: omnichannel retail and 3D printing turn objects into downloads.
  • Price is becoming participative (PWYW, freemium), but sustainability and profitability need guardrails.

Key frameworks: The 4Ps · Co-creation 2×2 · ELM · Stage-gate

Course 2

Digital Media and Marketing Principles

Big idea: Digital media shifted marketing from one-way mass communication to two-way mass personalisation, and from persuasion to relationship building.

  • Treat every marketing action as communication with a purpose and an audience.
  • The big shift is from mass communication to mass personalisation.
  • Digital communication is interactive, networked, personalised and always on, and that changes information, persuasion and branding.
  • Power moved to the demand side: consumers and influencers now hold credibility.
  • Attention is the scarce resource, so context and timing matter as much as content.

Key frameworks: Communication model · Marketing funnel · PESO · Uses & gratifications

Course 3

Digital Media and Marketing Strategies

Big idea: Tactics and tools change; the core question 'say what, to whom, via what, for what purpose' never does.

  • Start every campaign with who you are, who your target is, what your goal is and what your message is.
  • Align communication strategy with marketing goals and business objectives.
  • Paid, organic, content, video and CRM work best as one integrated system.
  • Influence comes from relevance and credibility, not only reach.
  • Online PR is reputation management at every touchpoint.

Key frameworks: Four strategic questions · Johari window · PESO / omnichannel · CPM · CPC · CPA

Course 4

Digital Marketing Analytics in Theory

Big idea: Analysts combine strategy, technology and design to turn the data exhaust of digital channels into decisions, while the measurement ground shifts under privacy rules.

  • A great analyst blends strategy, technical skill and design, driven by curiosity, optimism and pride in detail.
  • Analytics has a long lineage (Snow, Minard), and the digital ad economy made data the internet's currency.
  • Each channel (video, search, display, social) produces different signals; search uniquely reveals intent.
  • The CDJ is the organising framework for where marketing can influence behaviour.
  • Measure brand, outcomes, customer value and attribution together.

Key frameworks: Art + science mind · Consumer Decision Journey · MMM vs MTA · Density × ease of use

Course 5

Digital Marketing Analytics in Practice

Big idea: Good analysis is a disciplined journey from a clear objective to a visual story, and every detail of the visual carries meaning.

  • Follow a repeatable process: plan, collect, analyse, report, then repeat with what you learned.
  • Revenue is a result; analysis should target one of six marketing objectives.
  • Prefer facilitated downloads, then APIs, then scraping, and always check for bias.
  • Look for change, clustering, relativity, ranking and correlation, and give each its context.
  • Good charts need clear meaning, deliberate contrast and polish.

Key frameworks: Plan · Collect · Analyse · Report · Objectives hierarchy · 5 analysis types · Good visual form

Course 6

The Digital Marketing Revolution

Big idea: Digital doesn't simply replace analog. Marketers win by knowing which benefits customers value and mixing analog and digital on purpose.

  • Digital doesn't always win. Map the eight benefits to see which one a customer actually values.
  • Digital dominates through efficiency and democracy.
  • Analog resists through physicality and protection, and coexists with digital.
  • The best plays often combine both: glocal reach and TouchTech products.
  • Adding digital (embracing) or removing it (distancing) can transform an analog offering.

Key frameworks: 8 benefits · 4 relationships · 8 key concepts · Webrooming / showrooming
