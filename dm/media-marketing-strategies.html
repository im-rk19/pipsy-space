Digital Media and Marketing Strategies

Planning, executing and evaluating a digital campaign end to end.

Prof. Mike Yao4 modulesM1 Preparing for success · M2 Discovering & reaching consumers · M3 Engaging & growing consumers · M4 Evaluation & risk management

Summary

The applied follow-on to Principles. The whole course hangs on one strategic question: what do you want to say, to whom, through which channels, and for what purpose?

It moves through campaign preparation (self-assessment, targeting, goals, message and website), reaching consumers (paid ads, SEO, content, video, CRM), engaging and growing them (social, influencer, online PR, email) and finally evaluation and risk (KPIs, ad fraud, privacy, brand safety). Each module ends with an industry expert interview.

Big idea. Tactics and tools change; the core question 'say what, to whom, via what, for what purpose' never does.

Module notes

Module 1

Preparing for success

Know yourself, your target, your goal, your message
Know yourself
A Johari-window self-assessment: who you think you are, how others see you, who you want to be, and whether you have the resources to get there (Airbnb is a tech firm to itself, a cheap hotel to users).
Know your target
Digital lets you find consumers through data rather than guessing from mass-media audiences. Big data is the 'fossil fuel' of the digital economy; let the question choose the method.
Know your goal
Align communication strategy → marketing goals → business objectives. Journeys are non-linear, so think of a tour guide or personal assistant rather than a funnel.
Know your message
Coherent, consistent content across fragmented channels; a mix of paid, owned, earned and shared in an omnichannel plan.
Website as core asset
Judge it on findability, accessibility, desirability, usability, credibility and usefulness. Communicate consistently and use digital asset management (DAM).

Case / expert

Mary McIlrath (C+R Research): researching kids and families with age-appropriate, activity-based methods.

Practical tips

  • Big data and machine learning only give the right answers if you ask the right questions.
Module 2

Discovering & reaching consumers

Paid, organic, content, video, CRM
Paid advertising
Display, search (PPC), social, e-commerce and addressable TV ads. Used both for direct response and for brand awareness.
Pricing models
CPM (per 1,000 impressions) for awareness, CPC/PPC per click, CPA per acquisition for conversion. Without targeting, the true cost per acquisition balloons.
SEO
Technical (crawlable, indexed, linked pages), on-site (the right keywords, not just brand names) and off-site (quality inbound links, guest blogging). Increasingly also optimising for voice assistants and recommenders.
Content marketing
Attention is scarce (Herbert Simon), so earn it with useful content in many formats across owned, streaming and social channels (Blue Apron's cooking education).
Online video
A separate strategy from TV: fragmented audiences, organic and paid formats, a branded channel.
CRM & marketing automation
A single customer database tracks every interaction, avoids duplicate outreach and powers personalised email. Sync marketing automation with sales.

Case / expert

AMOBEE executives: programmatic moved from cheap remnant inventory to premium Fortune-500 spending; the job needs constant curiosity.

Practical tips

  • Match the pricing model to the objective.
  • Build different keyword sets for different campaigns and landing pages.
Module 3

Engaging & growing consumers

Social, influencer, PR, email
Social media strategy
Treat social media as a parallel universe with its own norms. Three strategies: social advertising, social content marketing and influencer marketing.
Buzz, eWOM & influencers
Andreasen's four information sources (impersonal or personal × advocate or independent). Influencers are the common core of buzz and word-of-mouth.
Influencer selection
Reach, relevance and credibility. Engagement falls as follower counts rise; the 10K–100K tier balances reach and engagement best. Micro-influencers win in niches.
Online PR
Context collapse: many audiences share one space and brands are discussed beyond their control. Maximise favourable third-party mentions and monitor reputation.
Email marketing
Still powerful and branding even when deleted, but intrusive if unsolicited. Use house lists, co-branded and third-party newsletters; respect privacy and give control.

Case / expert

Lynne McChristian: reputations are built slowly and lost fast; keep pushing good news out and use your experts for visibility.

Research insights

  • Industry data cited: influencer content shows far higher ROI than other digital formats.

Practical tips

  • Set goals before choosing influencers.
  • Disclose sponsorships; mismatched influencer campaigns hurt both parties.
Module 4

Evaluation & risk management

KPIs, fraud, privacy, brand safety
Website KPIs
Traffic, source, bounce, time on site and above all conversion rate, defined by site type (purchase, sign-up, lead). Analysts track detail; managers watch high-level KPIs.
Social KPIs
Go beyond follower counts to engagement, reach, share of voice, sentiment and conversions, read in platform and cultural context (20K likes means less on WeChat).
Ad fraud
Pixel stuffing, domain spoofing and bots. Fight it with publisher outreach, strategic buying, third-party blocking and pre-bid filtering.
Privacy & data security
GDPR-style regulation is reshaping targeting. Transparency builds trust even when people are worried about privacy.
Brand safety & reputation
Programmatic buying reduces control over ad placement. Plan remediation in advance and keep an authentic, consistent identity.

Case / expert

Jill Ferguson (Chartbeat): content intelligence and recirculation; getting readers to page 2 and 3 builds loyalty.

Practical tips

  • Measure to improve, not to justify.
  • Integrate: ads bring people to a storefront that must be well organised.

Key takeaways

  1. Start every campaign with who you are, who your target is, what your goal is and what your message is.
  2. Align communication strategy with marketing goals and business objectives.
  3. Paid, organic, content, video and CRM work best as one integrated system.
  4. Influence comes from relevance and credibility, not only reach.
  5. Online PR is reputation management at every touchpoint.
  6. Choose KPIs that reflect your real goals, and plan for fraud, privacy and brand-safety risks.
  7. Don't chase shiny tools; keep the strategic question at the centre.
