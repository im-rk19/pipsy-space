Digital Marketing Analytics in Theory

The mindset, history, channels and measurement science behind marketing analytics.

Prof. Kevin Hartman4 modulesM1 The day the geeks took over · M2 Digital channels & the consumer journey · M3 The science of analytics · M4 Tools, maturity & privacy

Summary

Taught by a former Google analytics lead, this course builds the conceptual base for marketing analytics: the 'art + science' mindset an analyst needs, how data analysis evolved from early maps to the digital ad economy, and how each major channel produces data.

It then covers the science of measurement (data infrastructure, brand metrics, consumer outcomes, customer value, attribution) and closes on the tool landscape, organisational maturity and the privacy wave that is cutting off cookies.

Big idea. Analysts combine strategy, technology and design to turn the data exhaust of digital channels into decisions, while the measurement ground shifts under privacy rules.

Module 1

The day the geeks took over

Mindset and history of analytics
Art + science mind
Three roles: data strategist (links data to business impact), techie (collects and processes data) and data designer (communicates it). Three attitudes: curiosity, optimism and 'swagger', i.e. pride and attention to detail.
Eight epochs of early history
From early maps and diagrams (before 1600), measurement and theory, new graphic forms, modern graphics and a golden age to the 'dark ages', rebirth and high-definition analytics. Icons: John Snow's cholera map and Minard's Napoleon chart.
Contemporary history (1994–2014)
From AT&T's first banner ad, through the dot-com bust, to the 'seeds of prosperity' (LinkedIn, Facebook, Twitter), social for good, and 'analytics' becoming mainstream.
Modern analytics
Digital ads fund the free internet. P&G's first and second 'moments of truth', smartphones everywhere (the 2005 vs 2013 papal crowds), and regulation like GDPR, CCPA and browser tracking prevention.

Practical tips

  • Expect to fail often; optimism keeps the investigation going.
Module 2

Digital channels & the consumer journey

Video, search, display, social, CDJ
Online video
Sight, sound and motion; YouTube dominates while streaming services multiply. Formats range from skippable in-stream to sound-off social feed video.
Online search
Perhaps the greatest ad product ever: it reveals intent. Google dominates and 'near me' searches are surging. A results page has the query, text ads, shopping ads and organic results.
Display & programmatic
Banners, rich media, video and sponsorships traded algorithmically in real time across a complex ad-tech stack (the LUMA landscape). Cookie loss threatens targeting.
Social media
Around six leading platforms, each filling a different need by age group. Ad formats converge because platforms copy each other; engagement is the key ROI measure.
Consumer Decision Journey (McKinsey)
Initial consideration → active evaluation → moment of purchase → post-purchase experience → loyalty loop. Maps to P&G's first and second moments of truth.

Case / expert

Federal Moto (a local motorcycle shop): paid search keywords drive business and show which days and terms work.

Practical tips

  • Data can look objective while carrying a point of view; tell the bigger story honestly.
Module 3

The science of analytics

Infrastructure, brand, outcomes, value, attribution
Digital data infrastructure
Cookies, tags and sign-ins collect data. Measurement gives understanding and accountability across four categories: brand, outcomes, customer value and attribution.
Brand measurement
Brand lift (awareness, consideration, favourability, intent), viewability, verification (right context), fraud checks and Net Promoter Score.
Consumer outcomes
Sales, revenue, market share and store visits. Straightforward for pure digital businesses, harder for CPG; uses claimed (survey) data and objective hierarchies (business → marketing → media → campaign).
Customer value
Different customers are worth different amounts (airline loyalty tiers). Assign value to micro-actions like newsletter sign-ups to guide strategy.
Attribution
Media Mix Models use aggregated, impression-based data; Multi-Touch Attribution uses user-level journeys. Privacy rules push both towards probabilistic modelling.

Practical tips

  • Set objectives at business, marketing, media and campaign level so each metric ladders up.
Module 4

Tools, maturity & privacy

Choosing tools and the regulation wave
Tool categories by data density
Enterprise platforms (Adobe, Google Analytics 360, IBM), point solutions, and analysis gadgets for light needs.
Ease of use
A personal, changing metric. Plot tools on density × ease of use, find one go-to tool for about 75% of your work (Hartman's is Tableau) and master it.
Digital marketing maturity
BCG's stages: nascent → emerging → connected → multi-moment. Higher maturity brings better results and frees analysts; most firms sit in the middle.
Privacy
Consumer worry (plus some sensationalism) drives regulation like GDPR and CCPA. Cookie deletion breaks measurement and retargeting, and platforms are starting to act like publishers.

Practical tips

  • Invest in people first, then equip them with tools, not the other way round.

Key takeaways

  1. A great analyst blends strategy, technical skill and design, driven by curiosity, optimism and pride in detail.
  2. Analytics has a long lineage (Snow, Minard), and the digital ad economy made data the internet's currency.
  3. Each channel (video, search, display, social) produces different signals; search uniquely reveals intent.
  4. The CDJ is the organising framework for where marketing can influence behaviour.
  5. Measure brand, outcomes, customer value and attribution together.
  6. Choose tools by data density and ease of use, then master one go-to tool.
  7. Privacy regulation is eroding cookies, pushing measurement towards modelling.
